## V3 prompt

Create a NEW accurate educational crochet slip-knot illustration, not a variation of the faulty knot image in conversation. Use a portrait white sheet, turquoise yarn, silver crochet hook, precise ink outlines and light shading. FOUR stacked horizontal close-up panels. Large drawings, minimal text. Teach the common method of placing a crossed yarn loop IN FRONT OF a straight section of working yarn, inserting hook through loop, catching working yarn behind loop, pulling up a new loop, then tightening old loop around it. TOPOLOGY CONSTRAINT: ONE continuous cord with exactly TWO ends; no branching, no cord fused into another. Make yarn thin enough to trace all crossings. STEP1: form a simple circular loop on table with one crossing at bottom; short tail exits down-right and working strand exits down-left then extends back UP BEHIND centre of original loop. Label tail and working yarn. STEP2: a metal crochet hook comes from above through the open original loop and its open tip catches the straight WORKING strand BEHIND the loop. Do NOT draw an already formed bight or a second ring at this step. This is a new unambiguous intermediate view: a single straight strand behind the ring, caught by the hook. STEP3: hook draws a fold of that working strand forwards through the original ring; show original ring loose around the neck of pulled-through bight, distinct two legs and correct over/under occlusion. STEP4: original ring tightened into an actual simple overhand slip knot below the hook, one adjustable loop on hook, two distinct strands leaving knot (working strand to yarnball side upper left and short tail down-right). Exact panel captions: '1. Make a loop'; '2. Catch the working yarn behind the loop'; '3. Pull a new loop through'; '4. Adjust gently on the hook'. Bottom note: 'Keep the tail outside. The adjustable loop should slide on the hook.' Draw the same SINGLE yarn path progressing in time. Do not copy previous inaccurate panel. Render a teaching diagram with physically traceable yarn, no impossible closed torus, no extraneous loops, no hands covering the path. No title needed besides panel labels.
