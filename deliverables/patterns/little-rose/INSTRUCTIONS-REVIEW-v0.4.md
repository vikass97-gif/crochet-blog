# Little Rose - editorial revision v0.4

19 October 2026. English / US crochet terms. Working text for the next PDF; not physically tested. This revision does not replace the downloadable v0.3 PDF. No measured dimensions, yarn quantities or making time are claimed.

## Before starting

You need chain (ch), slip stitch (sl st), single crochet (sc), half double crochet (hdc), double crochet (dc), and basic hand sewing. Use smooth yarn in a petal colour and a leaf colour, a compatible crochet hook, removable markers, scissors and a yarn needle. Record the exact yarn and hook used. No yarn/hook combination has yet been approved for this construction.

The active loop on the hook and the slip knot do not count as chains or stitches. The ch 1 before Row 2 does not count as a stitch. Work Row 2 under both top loops of each Row 1 stitch. Foundation-chain insertion is described separately below.

## Rose foundation

1. Leave a starting tail suitable for weaving into the finished base. Make a slip knot and ch 25.
2. Starting from the hook, identify the first completed chain directly below the active loop. Skip this chain; insert into the second completed chain. Work 1 sc there, then 1 sc into each remaining chain: **24 sc**. Use one consistent strand of the chain's front V; do not work into the slip knot. Photograph the chosen insertion method for review.
3. Count the 24 stitch tops before continuing. Do not add or skip stitches to conceal a counting error.
4. Ch 1 and turn. The first stitch you will work into is the **last sc made in Row 1**. This is now position 1 for Row 2.
5. From that working end, place removable markers in the tops of stitches **4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24**. These mark the last stitch of each four-stitch group. Do not number the groups from the opposite end before turning. Remove each marker when working into its stitch.

## Rose petal row

Work this sequence six times:

**[1 sl st in next st; 5 dc in next st; 5 dc in next st; 1 sl st in next st] x 6.**

For the first repeat:

1. Work 1 sl st into position 1, directly below the turning chain. Do not work into the turning chain.
2. Work all 5 dc into position 2. Reinsert into the same base stitch for every dc in this group.
3. Move to position 3 and work all 5 dc into that stitch. This is a different base stitch from position 2.
4. Remove the marker from position 4 and work 1 sl st into that stitch.

You have used four base stitches and made ten dc plus two sl st. Continue into the next unworked Row 1 stitch, not into any stitch just made in Row 2. Between repeats, the ending sl st and the next starting sl st go into **two separate adjacent base stitches**.

| Repeat | Row 1 positions used | Cumulative dc | Cumulative sl st |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1-4 | 10 | 2 |
| 2 | 5-8 | 20 | 4 |
| 3 | 9-12 | 30 | 6 |
| 4 | 13-16 | 40 | 8 |
| 5 | 17-20 | 50 | 10 |
| 6 | 21-24 | 60 | 12 |

Checkpoint: all 24 base stitches are used once as a base position; total new stitches = **72**, excluding ch 1. “Six repeats” describes the written grouping, not a physically confirmed count of visible petals after rolling.

Leave a sewing tail, cut the yarn and pull the cut end completely through the active loop to fasten off. Record the tail length and whether it was sufficient. Do not force a curling strip flat.

## Optional leaf

Ch 7. For this explanation, label the six available chain positions **A to F**, beginning with the second chain from the hook. F is the chain nearest the slip knot. A-F are position labels, not extra stitches.

Work into one consistent strand on the first side of the chain, leaving strands available on the opposite side:

- A: 1 sc.
- B: 1 hdc.
- C: 1 dc.
- D: 1 dc.
- E: 1 hdc.
- F: 3 sc, all in the same chain.

Rotate the work around the F end to reach the unused strands on the opposite side. Keep the same face visible; do not flip the piece as for a back-and-forth row. Do not ch 1. Do not work into the slip knot, add stitches to F, or crochet into the tops of the first-side stitches.

Return through the unused strands of **E, D, C, B, A**, in that order:

- E: 1 hdc.
- D: 1 dc.
- C: 1 dc.
- B: 1 hdc.
- A: 1 sc.

Join with a sl st into the top of the very first sc made at A. Count **13 stitches before the joining sl st**: 5 on the first side, 3 at F, 5 on the return side. The joining sl st does not count. Fasten off with a sewing tail.

The outline at both ends and the ease of this join still require testing. Do not add a tip or extra end stitches without recording a construction change and retesting the count.

## Rolling and assembly

1. Identify the original foundation-chain edge: this is the base to sew. The outer edges of the dc groups form the decorative edge and should remain free.
2. Lay the strip so both ends are visible. Start at the end **opposite the long finishing tail**. Roll the first group to form a centre, then wrap the rest around it while keeping the foundation edges together underneath. Do not fasten the petals tightly together at the top.
3. Hold the shape and check the view from above and below. If the strip cannot form a stable flower, photograph the problem and record it before altering the instructions. No reference sample establishes the intended degree of tightness yet.
4. Thread the finishing tail. From underneath, make small sewing passes through the stacked foundation edges, changing direction so each layer is caught. Keep the upper petals free. Gently check the centre and outer wrap for movement; add passes where a layer remains loose.
5. Position the leaf with its A/join end under the rose and its F end projecting out. Sew through the leaf's starting end and the rose base. Check the position before tightening.
6. Weave the remaining ends through the base stitches. Trim only after checking the attachment. Record how the ends were secured and the lengths required. Decorative assembly checks do not constitute a product-safety or durability certification.
